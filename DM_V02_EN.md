## Overview

**Dark Meter (DM‑V02)** is the official reference version of the Dark‑Matter module within the *Monistic Continuum Model (MCM)*.  
It describes dark‑matter‑like behaviour as an expression of **pre‑spatial stress and curvature geometry** inside a monistic medium.

All observable phenomena are interpreted as **projections of bound and unbound geometries**, without relying on classical spacetime or particle concepts.

DM‑V02 refines the foundational ideas of V01 by introducing:

- a clarified ontology of **stress‑driven geometric reinforcement**,  
- a more precise distinction between **bound** and **unbound** geometric domains,  
- and an updated treatment of **rotational‑shear structures** that generate gravitational‑like effects at cosmological scales.

---

## Abstract

Dark Meter (DM‑V02) interprets dark‑matter‑like behaviour as a natural consequence of the continuum’s internal geometry.  
It does not rely on particle hypotheses or field constructs. Instead, it describes:

- **global stress redistribution** as the driver of large‑scale dynamics,  
- **geometric reinforcement zones** acting like mass concentrations,  
- **defect‑driven spatial growth** shaping emergent domains,  
- **rotational‑stress structures** producing gravitational‑like effects,  
- **long‑range geometric coupling** without external interaction fields.

DM‑V02 provides the link between local defect geometry and global cosmological behaviour.

## Scientific Classification

DM‑V02 represents a **highly complex, purely theoretical structure** with exceptional formal rigor and internal consistency.  
Its evaluation requires expertise in:

- ontological models,  
- abstract operator algebras,  
- non‑physical geometries.

The theory is **mathematically closed**, but **not yet empirically verifiable**, and is therefore classified as **fundamental research in pre‑spatial ontology**.

## Core Concepts

### Pre‑Spatial Medium  
A monistic continuum without fundamental spacetime, fields, or particles.

### Stress Geometry  
Global and local stress redistribution forming reinforcement zones.

### Bound vs. Unbound Geometry  
- **Bound:** stable geometric structures  
- **Unbound:** dissolution, growth, and long‑range coupling

### Rotational‑Shear Structures  
Geometric modes producing gravitational‑like effects.

### Defect‑Driven Cosmology  
Spatial domains grow through stabilization of geometric defects.
